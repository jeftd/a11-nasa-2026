Before harmonization

Two sensors, one fire record

The challenge asks for a burning-activity calendar from 2000 to today. MODIS covers the whole period; VIIRS is the future. Before merging them we need to know what each number means and why the two sensors disagree. Every figure on this page comes from FIRMS data we analyzed (2024, Mexico and the globe).

1 · What a hotspot is (and is not)

A hotspot is one satellite pixel that the detection algorithm flagged as containing at least one active fire at the moment of the overpass. FIRMS gives its center coordinates, time, size and intensity.

  • It is a snapshot, not a fire event. One wildfire produces many hotspots over many pixels and passes.
  • The location is the pixel center, not the exact flame position.
  • No hotspot ≠ no fire: the place may have been cloudy, not observed, or the fire too small.
  • Do not estimate burned area from hotspot counts (FIRMS recommendation).

3 · Why MODIS and VIIRS counts differ

Raw counts differ by about 4–5×. That gap is not one thing but the product of several effects — each one has to be handled (or at least understood) by the harmonization.

a

Pixel size → splitting

MODIS pixel ≈ 2.06 km² on average, VIIRS ≈ 0.23 km² (9× smaller). Where both see the same fire, one MODIS hotspot ≈ 2–3 VIIRS hotspots.

b

Sensitivity → extra small fires

70% of VIIRS hotspots have no MODIS hotspot within 2 km. They are small, cool fires (median FRP 6 MW vs 26 MW for MODIS).

c

Overpass time

Terra (~10:30) catches morning fires, Aqua and VIIRS (~12:40–13:30) the afternoon peak. In 2024 Aqua flagged 2.3× more hotspots than Terra with the same instrument.

d

Number of satellites

VIIRS went from 1 satellite (2012) to 2 (2018), 3 (2024) and back to 2 (Nov 2026). Summing satellites creates fake jumps: Mexico 105k → 343k → 517k hotspots/yr.

e

Coverage & swath edge

One MODIS satellite sees the equator ~1.8×/day with gaps; VIIRS 2.2×/day, none. MODIS edge pixels grow to ~2 × 4.8 km; VIIRS stays small.

f

Day vs night

Night share of detections: Terra 40%, Aqua 6%, VIIRS 24%. Night fires are cooler and VIIRS detects them better. Day and night should be analyzed consistently.

Same fires? Mostly the big ones. With Aqua vs Suomi NPP (same afternoon), 73% of MODIS hotspots have a VIIRS hotspot within 2 km, but only 30% of VIIRS hotspots have a MODIS one. VIIRS reports 5.6× more pixels yet flags ~40% less total area.

4 · Same fires, two pixel grids

A schematic 3 × 3 km area seen by MODIS (1 km pixels) and VIIRS (375 m pixels). Fires are drawn to scale. A pixel is flagged when the fire area inside it exceeds a fixed fraction of the pixel (0.1% — FIRMS says MODIS routinely detects fires of ~1,000 m² in a 1 km² pixel). Illustrative rule, not the real algorithm.

Try small fires: VIIRS flags them, MODIS does not (sensitivity). Try a few large fires: both flag them, but VIIRS splits each into several pixels (splitting) while MODIS flags more total area.

Planned pipeline

  1. Filter consistentlytype = 0, same confidence policy, local dates, outage days marked missing.
  2. Fix the satellite setMODIS = Terra + Aqua; VIIRS reference = NOAA-20 (continuity); S-NPP to extend the overlap to 2012.
  3. Count fire cells / fire dayson a common grid, per day, per sensor.
  4. Calibrate on the overlapk(region, calendar month) = VIIRS / MODIS, 2012/2018 → 2025, with uncertainty.
  5. Build the seriesVIIRS where available, calibrated MODIS before; flag provenance.

Glossary

Overpass
One satellite passing over a place; all hotspots it flags at that moment.
Swath
Strip of ground imaged in one pass (MODIS 2,330 km, VIIRS 3,040 km).
Nadir / swath edge
Directly below the satellite / the sides of the swath, where pixels are larger.
FRP
Fire Radiative Power, MW. Proxy of fire intensity; not directly comparable across sensors.
Overlap period
Years when MODIS and VIIRS both operate (2012 → today) — used to calibrate.
NRT vs SP
Near-real-time (~3 h, provisional) vs standard processing (science quality, ~5-month lag).
Local solar time
Time of day from the Sun's position: UTC + longitude / 15 h.
