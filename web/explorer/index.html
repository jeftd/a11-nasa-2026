Two instruments, five satellites

MODIS and VIIRS are instruments — thermal cameras. MODIS flies on two NASA satellites since 2000/2002; VIIRS flies on three NOAA/NASA satellites since 2012. All still operate except Suomi NPP, which stopped sending data on 2 November 2026.

What one reading contains

Each row of a FIRMS file is one fire pixel — a pixel the algorithm flagged as containing at least one fire. Below are real records from 2024. Hover a field to read what it means.

Same 4 fires, different counts

One MODIS pixel (~1 km) containing 4 small fires produces 1 hotspot. VIIRS divides the same area into ~7 × 7 375 m pixels, so it can report 4 hotspots — and it also catches smaller, cooler fires that MODIS misses.

The overlap: why harmonization is needed — and possible

Monthly vegetation-fire hotspots in Mexico, 2000–2025, per satellite. VIIRS counts are about 4× MODIS for the same months, yet the curves rise and fall together. The years when both observe at the same time (2012 →) are the overlap: they let us learn the conversion between sensors and apply it to the MODIS-only years (2000–2011).

The problem

Adding up “all satellites” creates a fake jump in 2012 and 2018 — it looks as if fires multiplied, but only the number and type of sensors changed. Toggle Yearly to see the naive total.

The clue

In 2019–2024 the MODIS and NOAA-20 monthly series correlate at r ≈ 0.995. They see the same fire seasons; only the scale differs (3.2× – 5.9× by month).

The fix

Learn the ratio per region and calendar month from the overlap, convert MODIS-era counts to VIIRS-equivalent, and show one continuous 2000 → today calendar.
