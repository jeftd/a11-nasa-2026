How the record is harmonized

Why a ratio. VIIRS (375 m) sees smaller fires than MODIS (1 km), so it reports several times more hotspots for the same fires, but both follow the same season. Scaling MODIS to the VIIRS level of each area puts 25 years on one scale.

Uncertainty. MODIS-era months carry a low–high range from the spread of the yearly ratios (10th–90th percentile).

What's in the files?

Hotspots CSV: one row per satellite detection (vegetation fires) inside the area and range, all satellites: acq_date, acq_time (UTC HHMM), latitude, longitude, sensor, satellite, confidence, conf_class, frp, brightness, bright_2, scan, track, daynight, fire_type, processing, version. Up to 200,000 rows.

Monthly CSV: one row per month in the range: area, year, month, count, sensor_era, harmonized, low, high, baseline_median, percentile, category.

About the data

Hotspots are satellite detections, not confirmed fires. They include agricultural burns and will not match official fire counts.

One consistent record. MODIS-era years are adjusted to the VIIRS scale so years can be compared; those years carry an estimate range. See the Method tab.

"Normal" means the middle third of the same month in the baseline years, for the same days when the month is still in progress.

Source: NASA FIRMS (MODIS C6.1, VIIRS 375 m). We acknowledge the use of data from NASA's Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS), part of NASA's LANCE and ESDIS.
